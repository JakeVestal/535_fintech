What you are building

A covered call: long 100 shares, short 1 call. Backtest it on one name. Simulate execution. Track a Reg T account. Publish a clean blotter of every entry and exit.

You must have clear, algorithmic rules for entering and for exiting (roll, buy-to-close, or expire). Complexity is optional. A baseline is acceptable:

Baseline: Monday open or Monday close, buy the covered call as a combo / basket (100 shares + short 1 call) with Friday expiry (~5 DTE). Limit orders. Fill the call at the bid/ask midpoint. Hold to Friday. If you add a roll rule, write it down and run it.

Pick your underlying

Any U.S. equity or ETF you can pull from LSEG. Prefer a name with liquid weeklies so mids are tight and last prices actually print — AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, SPY, QQQ, etc. The worked example uses AAPL.

Fills and limit orders

Work limit orders (easier to model than market orders).

  • Default, this assignment: filled at option mid = (BID + ASK) / 2 at the order timestamp. Combined with intraday stock prints, you can price the combo any time of day.
  • Optional: fit a volatility curve to multiple prints, infer a fair price, assume that fill — and show the fit.

No bid/ask at that bar → no fill. Skip. Do not invent a print.

What LSEG gives you on expired options

Intraday history exists, field set is limited. Expect:

  • BID and ASK (treat as always-on for this assignment)
  • When present: TRDPRC_1, OPEN_PRC, HIGH_1, LOW_1, ACVOL_UNS, NUM_MOVES

That is enough. Use the same bar size for stock and options (hourly default for ~10 weeks).

Fetch near-the-money calls along the chain (strikes from a bit below the stock’s low in the window to a bit above the high). Graph TRDPRC_1 on Y vs MID=(BID+ASK)/2 on X, fit a line, report R². That justifies the midpoint assumption on your name.

Entry / exit (algorithmic)

  1. Entry rule a machine could run. Baseline: Monday open or close, combo, Friday weekly, strike you specify (ATM, nearest OTM, delta, … — say it).
  2. Exit rule. Expire, BTC, assignment, or roll — a rule, not a vibe.
  3. Roll is optional. If you implement one: when, which expiry/strike, what limit.

Blotter (non-negotiable)

Every entry and exit: time, instrument, side (BUY / SELL / BTC / EXPIRE / ASSIGN), qty, limit/fill, cash delta, note pointing at the rule that fired. Working orders do not belong here. A signal chart is not a blotter. The ledger is the running position: shares, short calls, cash, marks.

Reg T — what you must track

Use Reg T, not portfolio margin: computable without a broker PM engine, not broker-dependent; a book that lives in Reg T lives in PM, not the reverse.

Covered call (long 100, short 1 call on those shares):

  • NAV = cash + stock MV + option MV (short call is negative MV)
  • LMV = shares × stock mark
  • Initial margin = 50% of stock LMV. Covered short call adds $0
  • Maintenance = 25% of stock LMV (FINRA). Covered call adds $0
  • Available funds = NAV − initial
  • Excess equity = NAV − maintenance (margin-call line)
  • Cash moves only when the blotter says so

Plot NAV and margin with mouseover. If available funds go negative, you could not have put the trade on — say so.

Option RICs (expired contracts)

OPRA / Reuters code, as in helios/python/option_rics.py:

{ROOT}{MONTH}{DAY}{YY}{STRIKE}.U{ ^MONTHYY if expired }
  • ROOT — equity RIC before the dot (AAPL.O → AAPL)
  • MONTH — letter for expiry month and put/call:
JanFebMarAprMayJunJulAugSepOctNovDec
CallABCDEFGHIJKL
PutMNOPQRSTUVWX
  • DAY — not zero-padded (5, not 05)
  • YY — two-digit expiry year
  • STRIKE — strike × 100, 5 digits: 14.50 → 01450, 190 → 19000, 192.50 → 19250
  • .U — OPRA / US listed
  • Expired only: append ^{MONTH}{YY} with the same letter. Live contracts have no caret suffix

Class example (expired 14.5-strike 21 Aug 2026 UUUU call): UUUUH212601450.U^H26

AAPL example RICs: AAPLF52619000.U^F26 (5 Jun 2026 190C), AAPLG172620000.U^G26 (17 Jul 2026 200C), AAPLH72620500.U^H26 (7 Aug 2026 205C).

Guess-and-check must fail soft. Use last session-in-week from the stock tape so you do not invent holiday expiries.
