What I am grading

Analysis, thoughtfulness, and how you implemented the theory. Did you write a ~5 DTE covered call with explicit fill, roll, and pin rules? Does the blotter match those rules? Are Reg T numbers used like an account, not a decoration?

Strategy you implement

  1. One U.S. equity (Helios sample is UUUU — pick your own if you want).
  2. Buy 100 shares.
  3. Sell 1 call, target ~5 DTE (weeklies).
  4. Each week: expire, get assigned, or roll — your rules, written on the site.
  5. About 10 weeks of history. Strikes from a bit below the window low to a bit above the high.

The blotter is trades you actually booked, not a signal chart.

What the Pages site must contain

  • Blotter — time, instrument, side, qty, price, notes.
  • Ledger — stock, short calls, cash over time.
  • Reg T accounts — NAV, cash, initial (50% of stock LMV; the covered short call adds $0), maintenance (25% of LMV), available funds (NAV − initial), excess (NAV − maintenance).
  • NAV / margin path with mouseover values.
  • Write-up — fill rule, roll rule, pin/assignment, and why we use Reg T instead of portfolio margin (computable without a broker PM engine; not broker-dependent; a book that lives in Reg T lives in PM, not the other way around).
  • Optional but useful: mid = (bid+ask)/2 vs trade, with R².

How you get the data (ungraded)

Workspace open and signed in, from a clone of this repo:

python3 helios/python/local_server.py
# then http://127.0.0.1:8765/helios/data.html

Search, fetch hourly (or daily) bars, download JSON, bake what you used into book.js (or your equivalent), push gh-pages/. Prefer hourly over 1-minute for a 10-week window. One RIC at a time. Expired U.S. equity calls look like UUUUH212601450.U^H26.
