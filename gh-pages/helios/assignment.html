What you are building

A covered call backtest: long 100 shares, short 1 call. Backtest it on one stock of your choosing. I adivse you to pick an underlying that has liquid, easy-to-price options to make this easier. Simulate fills by pulling historical price data and using it to generate a blotter of what would have happened if your entry conditions were met. See the example page for guidance. You must have clear, algorithmic rules for entering a trade and for exiting a trade (roll, buy-to-close, or let it expire). A perfectly fine and acceptable set of rules that will get full credit for this assignment is would be to write a loop that scrolls through the historical data and records:

  1. Buy 100 shares at the stock price (reduce cash by the amount) on Monday
  2. Write 1 call, expiring that Friday at an OTM strike and increase your cash amount by the premium.
  3. Wait for expiry. If ITM at expiry? Then do the same thing for the next week. If not? Then write another call next Monday.

If you go more complex than this, I won't hold you back, but I suggest you at least start from this simple case (so that you're covered for your HW grade) before adding complexity on top of it.

As you do this, you will need to make all sorts of small but important design and financial engineering decisions. HOW will you choose the strike price at which you write the call? Why did you choose the stock that you did? Is it better to hold until expiry, or to immediately set a limit order to buy back the call at X% profit (and simulate a "fill" of that happening by writing down the cash payment to buy back)? Write down these decisions on your websites; that is your algorithm and that is the true point of this assignment -- to identify and communicate those decisions that determine the entry and exit rules of your strategy.

Pick your underlying

Any U.S. equity (or ETF) you can pull from LSEG. Prefer a name with liquid weeklies so mids are tight and TRDPRC_1 actually prints — AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, SPY, QQQ, etc. Thin names make the midpoint assumption harder to defend (but it's still suprisingly robust for many smaller equities).

Making your Blotter

  • Default, this assignment: you get filled at the option mid = (BID + ASK) / 2 at the timestamp of the order. Combined with intraday stock prints, that lets you price the covered-call combo any time of day.
  • Optional upgrade: fit a volatility curve to multiple prints, infer a fair price, and assume that fill. If you do this, show the fit.

If there is no bid/ask at that bar, you do not fill. Skip. Do not invent a print. This is the worst mistake an algo trader can make and it will cost you money in the long (or short) run.

Intraday history on expired listed options is available, but the field set is limited. You should expect:

  • when a trade happens, TRDPRC_1, OPEN_PRC, HIGH_1, LOW_1, ACVOL_UNS, NUM_MOVES.

    • That is enough. Mid from BID/ASK is the fill. Stock OHLC/intraday is the other half of the combo. Pull the same bar size for stock and options (hourly is the right default for a ~10-week window).

    Fetch near-the-money calls along the chain (same idea as the first homework: strikes from a bit below the stock’s low in the window to a bit above the high). Then graph TRDPRC_1 on Y against MID = (BID+ASK)/2 on X, fit a line, report R². That is how you justify the midpoint assumption on your name.

    Blotter (non-negotiable)

    Submit a clean blotter of trades you actually booked: entries and exits. Time, instrument (stock or option RIC / occ label), side (BUY / SELL / BTC / EXPIRE / ASSIGN), qty, limit/fill price, cash delta, notes that point at the rule that fired. Working orders do not belong here. A signal chart is not a blotter.

    The ledger is the running position: shares, short calls (strike + expiry), cash, marks.

    Reg T — what you must track

    Use a Reg T account, not portfolio margin. Reg T is computable without a broker’s PM/SPAN engine, it is not broker-dependent, and a book that survives Reg T survives PM — not the other way around.

    For a covered call (long 100 shares, short 1 call on those shares):

    • NAV (equity) = cash + stock market value + option market value. A short call is a negative MV.
    • Long market value (LMV) = shares × stock mark.
    • Initial margin = 50% of stock LMV. The covered short call adds $0 initial. (A naked short call would; you are not naked.)
    • Maintenance margin = 25% of stock LMV (FINRA). Again, the covered call does not add a naked-option maintenance pad.
    • Available funds = NAV − initial. Room to put on a new risk.
    • Excess equity = NAV − maintenance. The margin-call line.
    • Cash moves only when the blotter says so (buy stock, collect premium, pay BTC, expire at 0, assignment).

    Plot NAV (and initial / maintenance) over time with mouseover values. If available funds go negative, you could not have put the trade on — say so.

    Option RICs (how to ask LSEG for an expired contract)

    US listed equity options on OPRA use this Reuters Instrument Code. The Helios Python (helios/python/option_rics.py) builds it like this:

    {ROOT}{MONTH}{DAY}{YY}{STRIKE}{ .U }{ ^MONTHYY if expired }
    • ROOT — equity RIC before the dot, upper case. AAPL.O → AAPL. UUUU.N → UUUU.
    • MONTH — a single letter for the expiry month and put/call:
    JanFebMarAprMayJunJulAugSepOctNovDec
    CallABCDEFGHIJKL
    PutMNOPQRSTUVWX
    • DAY — expiry day, not zero-padded (5 not 05). Matches the Python: expiry.day.
    • YY — two-digit year of the expiry.
    • STRIKE — strike × 100, 5 digits, zero-padded. 14.50 → 01450. 190 → 19000. 192.50 → 19250.
    • .U — OPRA / US listed.
    • Expired only: append ^{MONTH}{YY} using the same month letter. Live contracts do not get the caret suffix.

    Class example (expired call): 14.5-strike, 21 Aug 2026, UUUU:

    UUUUH212601450.U^H26
ld.get_history(universe="UUUUH212601450.U^H26",
               fields=["TRDPRC_1", "BID", "ASK"],
               interval="hourly", start=..., end=...)

    AAPL worked-example RICs (expired as of this assignment):

    AAPLF52619000.U^F26     # AAPL 5 Jun 2026 190 call
AAPLG172620000.U^G26    # AAPL 17 Jul 2026 200 call
AAPLH72620500.U^H26     # AAPL 7 Aug 2026 205 call

    A put uses the put letter. Same strike/day/year/.U/suffix. Guess-and-check must fail soft (empty series, not a crash). Weeklys: take the last session in each week from the stock tape so you do not invent holiday expiries, then walk Friday from there.
